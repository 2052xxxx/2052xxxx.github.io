**1. Homepage (Your Professional Hook)**
This is the first thing people see. It should be clean, modern, and immediately communicate who you are and what you're looking for.

*   **Professional Headline:** "Mechanical Engineer Specializing in CAD Design & Finite Element Analysis" or "New Graduate Civil Engineer | Passionate about Sustainable Infrastructure Projects". Be specific.
*   **Your Best Photo:** A high-quality, friendly, and professional headshot.
*   **Short Bio:** 2-3 sentences summarizing your skills, degree, and career goals.
*   **Call to Action (CTA) Buttons:**
    *   "View My Projects" (links to portfolio)
    *   "Download My Resume" (have a PDF always ready)
    *   "Get in Touch" (links to contact form)

**2. About Page (Your Story - This is CRUCIAL for you)**
This is where you turn your resume into a narrative. You must address the 1-year gap proactively and positively.

*   **Your Story:** Write in the first person ("I", "me"). Start with your passion for engineering—why did you choose it?
*   **Education:** List your degree, university, graduation year, and relevant coursework (e.g., Thermodynamics, Structural Analysis, Fluid Mechanics). Mention any key projects or academic achievements.
*   **The Experience Section:**
    *   **Internship (3 months):** Don't just list duties. Use bullet points with **action verbs and quantifiable results**.
        *   *Instead of:* "Worked on CAD models."
        *   *Write:* "Designed and refined 15+ component models in SolidWorks, improving assembly efficiency by 10%."
    *   **The 1-Year Gap:** **FRAME THIS AS A "SKILL-BUILDING PERIOD" OR "PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT."** Be honest but strategic. Choose the narrative that fits best (you can combine them):
        *   **Narrative 1: Skill Development:** "Following my graduation, I dedicated myself to expanding my practical engineering skill set. This period involved intense self-directed learning and project work to complement my academic foundation."
        *   **Narrative 2: Career Exploration:** "I used this time to deeply research emerging fields within [Your Engineering Field] and identify the specific technical skills required to excel. This focused exploration allowed me to target my learning effectively."
*   **List the Skills You Gained:** This is the key! List the tools and skills you worked on during your gap.
    *   *Examples:* "Advanced my proficiency in **Python** for data analysis and automation," "Earned a certificate in **Project Management Fundamentals** from Coursera," "Mastered **ANSYS Simulation** software through online tutorials and personal projects."

**3. Portfolio / Projects Page (The Proof)**
This is the heart of your website. It proves you can *do* things, even without formal employment. Quantity is less important than quality.

*   **Personal Projects:** These are gold. They show initiative, passion, and practical application of skills.
    *   **Example 1 (CAD/FEA):** "Design and Static Stress Analysis of a Bike Frame" - Show your CAD model, screenshots of the mesh and stress results, and explain your process.
    *   **Example 2 (Coding):** "Python Script for Automating Data Collection" - Explain the problem, your solution, and link to the GitHub code.
    *   **Example 3 (Hands-On):** "3D-Printed Turbine Blade Design & Testing" - Show photos of the physical model, your design process, and test results.
*   **Academic Projects:** Feature your best university project. Treat it like a professional case study.
*   **Internship Project:** Showcase a significant piece of work from your internship (ensure you have permission to share it publicly and that it's not confidential).
*   **For Each Project, Use the STAR Method:**
    *   **Situation:** Briefly describe the context.
    *   **Task:** What was the goal?
    *   **Action:** What did **YOU** specifically do? (e.g., "I modeled the component in SolidWorks...," "I wrote the algorithm in Python to...").
    *   **Result:** What was the outcome? What did you learn?

**4. Skills Page (The ATS-Friendly List)**
Make it easy for recruiters and automated systems (Applicant Tracking Systems - ATS) to see your keywords.

*   Categorize them:
    *   **Technical Skills:** AutoCAD, SolidWorks, MATLAB, Python, ANSYS, LabVIEW, GD&T, etc.
    *   **Software:** MS Office Suite, LaTeX, etc.
    *   **Soft Skills:** Problem-Solving, Technical Communication, Project Management, Team Collaboration.

**5. Blog / Learning Journey Page (Highly Recommended)**
This is an *excellent* way to demonstrate passion and continuous learning, directly addressing the gap.

*   Write short articles (300-500 words) about:
    *   What you learned from a specific online course.
    *   A tutorial on how you solved a tricky technical problem in a personal project.
    *   Your analysis of a recent advancement in your engineering field.
*   This shows communication skills, deep thinking, and genuine interest.

**6. Contact Page**
Make it easy for them to reach you.

*   Simple contact form.
*   Links to your **professional** social media: **LinkedIn** (MUST HAVE), GitHub (if you code).
*   Your email address.

When asked, you will now have a powerful answer:

> "After graduating, I was very intentional about finding the right fit for my first engineering role. During that time, I wasn't idle. I focused on closing the gap between academia and industry by strengthening my practical skills. For example, you can see on my website I completed a course in [X] and applied it by building [Y project]. This period of self-directed learning gave me a deeper understanding of [Z skill] and confirmed my passion for this field. I'm now more prepared and motivated than ever to apply these skills to real-world challenges."

### Final Tips:

*   **Buy a Domain Name:** YourName.com or YourNameEngineering.com looks infinitely more professional than a free "wix.com/yourname" URL.
*   **Keep it Clean:** Use a simple, modern template. No flashy animations or hard-to-read fonts.
*   **Connect Everything:** Your resume, LinkedIn profile, and email signature should all link to your website.
*   **Be Proud:** You took initiative to build something. That itself is a valuable skill and a great talking point.

Your website transforms your story from "graduate with a gap" to "proactive, skilled, and passionate engineer who takes initiative." Good luck
